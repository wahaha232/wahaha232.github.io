How to play Chess

  • Each side has 16 pieces: a king, a queen, 2 rooks, 2 bishops, 2 knights and 8 pawns. White always moves first.
  • Pawns move forward one square (two on their first move) and capture diagonally. Rooks slide in straight lines, bishops slide diagonally, and the queen combines both. Knights jump in an L-shape and are the only piece that can leap over others.
  • Special moves are fully supported: castling (king and rook swap past each other for safety), en passant (a pawn can capture an enemy pawn that just moved two squares past it), and promotion (a pawn reaching the far rank becomes any other piece — usually a queen).
  • Putting the opponent's king under attack is check; if there's no way to escape it, that's checkmate and the game ends. If a player has no legal move but isn't in check, it's a stalemate — a draw.
  • The game can also end in a draw by insufficient material, the 50-move rule, or threefold repetition.
